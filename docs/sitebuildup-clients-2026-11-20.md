# What SiteBuildup needs to actually get clients

*2026-11-20 — market research, and the work that follows from it*

The site exists, ranks for nothing, and asks for nothing. Everything below is
either something the market says is being bought, or something the site is
missing that stops a stranger trusting it enough to send an enquiry.

Two sentences from that research matter more than the table:

- **"The middle 80% of legitimate UK quotes for a custom small business site land
  between £3,500 and £12,000"** — [Webkatie](https://webkatie.com/blog/small-business-website-cost-uk-2026).
  Anything below that range is bought from a page that explains itself; anything
  above it needs proof, a process and a contract.
- **Ongoing costs are the part clients forget and resent**: £100–£400/month to run
  a small site properly ([iWillDigital](https://iwilldigital.co.uk/blogs/how-much-does-a-website-design-cost-uk/)),
  £50–£200/month maintenance ([Glide Marketing](https://glidemarketing.co.uk/blog/how-much-should-a-website-cost-in-the-uk-in-2025/)).
  A retainer priced against those numbers is a normal thing to sell, not a push.

## What the market says wins the work

From the 2026 client-acquisition guides
([webdesigndev](https://webdesigndev.com/how-to-find-web-design-clients/),
[Website Profit Course](https://websiteprofitcourse.com/how-to-get-web-design-clients/),
[LPagery](https://lpagery.io/blog/get-web-design-leads/)), ranked by how well they
fit a one-person studio in Leeds:

1. **Google Business Profile.** Repeatedly named the single highest-return free
   channel for a local service business — it is what puts you in the map pack
   when somebody searches "web designer Leeds".
2. **Referrals, asked for deliberately.** Typical practice is a 10–15% referral
   fee, or a discount on future work; the ask works best immediately after a
   launch.
3. **A niche.** Every guide says the same thing: specialists charge more and get
   referred inside their niche. For SiteBuildup that means **music: DJs, venues,
   wedding suppliers, small labels** — the work already in the portfolio.
4. **Proof over adjectives.** Case studies with numbers ("checkout abandonment
   down 24%") instead of screenshots with adjectives.
5. **Build first, then pitch.** Redesign one page of a prospect's site and send it.
   The Studio can already audit a live URL and report what is broken — that is
   one click away from being the pitch.
6. **Local SEO on your own site.** A "[service] in [city]" page, citations, reviews.

## What the site is missing before any of that is sold

From the board in the Studio (`Standards` tab), sitebuildup.co.uk scores **28%** —
30 lines, 5 in place, 7 partly, 18 missing. The ones that block a sale, in order:

1. **No proof.** No case studies, no testimonials, no numbers. A stranger cannot
   check that you have done this before.
2. **No prices.** Every competitor in the table above publishes a range or a
   package. A site that says "contact for a quote" is read as expensive or vague.
3. **No Google Business Profile.** Nothing at all appears for a local search.
4. **No privacy notice or terms**, and the enquiry form collects names and emails.
5. **No booking link.** A DJ site without dates and availability cannot convert a
   wedding enquiry.
6. **No analytics**, so nothing is known about which page people leave from.
7. **No accessibility statement, no 404, no structured data.**

Each of those is a line on the Standards board with the two best examples of it
done well — the work is specified, not just named.

## The first thirty days

- **Week 1** — Google Business Profile claimed and completed; three case studies
  written (the band's site, the shop, this Studio) with one number each; prices
  published as packages.
- **Week 2** — privacy notice, terms, accessibility statement, and a 404; the
  enquiry form confirmed working end to end and answered within a day.
- **Week 3** — twenty prospects in the music niche, each audited with the Studio's
  own site check; send the audit, not a template.
- **Week 4** — three referral partners (a photographer, a wedding planner, a
  printer) with a written 10% offer; ask the two existing clients for a review.
